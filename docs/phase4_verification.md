# Phase 4 — Playable campaign integration

Status: playable engineering implementation and local verification complete. The user approved Prism/Gemini reviews on September 18, 2026. Two staged Phase 4 reviews and a targeted follow-up completed; their findings and disposition are below. The production acceptance gates below remain open.

The default client now connects five campaign acts to actual Core encounters and canonical permanent progression. The authored campaign contains fifteen main encounters, five regional bosses, five durable choices, six factions, six Greyhaven residents, and three optional exploration encounters. The combat overlay supplies regional roles, all eight elite modifiers, boss phases, guarded/recovery windows, and geometric warning areas. See `campaign_combat.md` and `campaign_contract.md` for the concrete mechanics and caps.

`CampaignRuntimeSession` coordinates the narrative ledger, active combat, and `ProductionSession` permanent inventory/services. Only actual enemy defeat advances an encounter and grants its payload-bound reward. Loot and mastery come from combat events. Revisit and duplicate-completion paths cannot grant a second reward. Anchors restore unfinished combat after death while retaining earned power and committed choices. Exploration timers, hazards, temporary population, and fragment effects end together when the context ends.

Greyhaven residents and services appear as rescues arrive. The map, story decisions, journal, anatomy, equipment, and crafting controls use public runtime commands. Region geometry distinguishes the five greybox environments while preserving the shared authoritative collision layout. Combat shows named enemies, elite modifiers, boss phases, and warning geometry. Story revelations are withheld until their authored discovery, and endings retain alliances, residents, city conditions, and the character's relationship to Resonance.

## Persistence and upgrade boundary

The campaign archive atomically joins story, permanent progression, active combat, exploration return context, RNG, and reward receipts. Restore verifies permanent/runtime projections and narrative/reward agreement. Raw schema, rules, and content checks precede strict typed parsing, preserving unfamiliar future archives. A separately locked profile ledger merges only discoveries and unlocks. Loading a merged profile establishes a new replay origin.

`aw campaign migrate-phase3 <source> <new-destination>` validates the original Phase 3 checksum and catalog, requires the character to be at Greyhaven, and imports retained equipment, XP, mastery, anatomy, services, and profile metadata into a fresh campaign story. It does not invent story choices or overwrite the source. Maintained fixtures contain an actual prior-build hub archive and its content catalogs with recorded byte hashes. Unsupported/in-flight characters remain intact for recovery in the prior build.

Act and encounter transitions restore exact snapshots, and final saves round-trip through the profile ledger.

## Prism review disposition

`prism-phase4.json` identified a warning-geometry edge case and the cost of per-command serialization. Fixed warning endpoints now clip to the authoritative arena bounds, and warning geometry may cross obstacles without disappearing. A regression exercises both furnace orientations in the smallest supported arena and restores their snapshots. The serialization allocation concern is real and measured above; every recorded command changes tick/arena state, so a dirty flag cannot remove its mandatory replay hash. Background hashing without immutable ownership would weaken correctness. Reducing snapshot copies remains a release-hardening optimization; measured p99 stays within budget.

`prism-phase4-final.json` identified repeated array growth when several permanent items are imported together. Reconciliation now imports the batch with one array allocation while retaining item order and deterministic seed/item-ID affix rolls. Its other findings were assessed: the proximity mask has at most eight hub interactions or one clue, never ground loot; actual interaction buttons use `interaction.Name`; exactly five acts is an explicit specification constraint; migration reserialization follows authentication of the original raw checksum before optional fields receive defaults.

The targeted `prism-phase4-item-import.json` response lacked surrounding definitions and reported two unsupported high findings: raw `CombatItem` has no permanent affix dictionary to overwrite, and `CombatItem` is a value-equality record, so its `SequenceEqual` check is valid. New-item filtering prevents a second affix roll. Godwrought mappings are validated ownership invariants and cannot be consumed by ordinary extraction; preserving a failed invariant is preferable to silently dropping ownership. Skill lookup is bounded by the authored 31-skill catalog and player ability events. Reward helpers do not write files: user operations have coordinator rollback, and archive writing validates the joined story/reward/projection before atomic replacement; process interruption does not persist an intermediate helper call.

After fixes, formatting and all 185 tests passed. The final batch-import change also passed the full-inventory runtime regression, rebuilt client, and actual exported 8,120-action campaign/replay with the unchanged hash above. Earlier scene and all-five-route evidence remains applicable: no authored content or supported-route combat outcome changed. The separate source and review reports are retained locally.
